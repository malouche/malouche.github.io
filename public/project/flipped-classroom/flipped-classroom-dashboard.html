📘 Research Overview

📄
Paper Summary

This study critically evaluates the Flipped Classroom (FC) model in an undergraduate social statistics course at Johns Hopkins University, examining how it impacts student engagement and inclusion in post-pandemic educational contexts.

Using a mixed-methods approach with 35 participants across Fall 2023 and Fall 2024, the research combines Likert-scale surveys with focus group discussions to assess both cognitive and affective engagement dimensions.

Key findings reveal that while FC provides foundational learning benefits, it presents significant limitations in fostering student autonomy and accommodating diverse learning preferences—challenges intensified by post-pandemic educational disruptions. The study identifies how Blended Learning (BL) design elements could better address these gaps through enhanced interaction, material diversification, and progressive scaffolding.

Context: Post-Pandemic Challenges

Following COVID-19, higher education faced unprecedented challenges in creating flexible yet rigorous learning environments. Remote learning had uneven impacts across student groups, creating variability in self-directed learning skills and highlighting structural inequities that require fundamental course redesign.

Purpose of the Study

To empirically evaluate the Flipped Classroom model's effectiveness in quantitative education and identify how Blended Learning design elements could better address diverse student needs through enhanced interactions, material diversification, and progressive scaffolding approaches.

Research Questions

RQ1: How does the design of online pre-class materials impact students' perceived learning and engagement in the remote portion?

RQ2: How do availability, diversification, and scaffolding of materials impact perceived relevance and engagement during in-class activities?

🧪 Methodology Explorer

Mixed-Methods Approach

This study employs a mixed-methods approach integrating quantitative and qualitative analyses to develop a comprehensive understanding of the FC model's effectiveness.

📊 Quantitative

Likert-scale surveys administered to all course participants, providing quantifiable data for statistical analysis with a 5-point scale (excluding neutral option).

💬 Qualitative

Focus groups conducted with voluntary participant subsets, capturing in-depth insights into nuanced opinions and emotional responses.

Analysis Methodology

Thematic analysis following Braun & Clarke's (2006) six-phase approach with intercoder reliability (Cohen's κ = 0.82).

🚀 Blended Learning Redesign Insights

Based on FC evaluation findings, the research identifies strategic BL approaches that could address identified limitations through interdependent learning components.

01

Enhanced Interaction Design

Address the 46% interaction gap by incorporating synchronous online discussion forums, virtual office hours, and peer collaboration tools as integral BL components rather than mere additions to pre-class activities.

Addresses: Limited Interaction (46%)
02

Material Diversification

Develop multiple content formats—videos, interactive simulations, case studies, and written materials—that accommodate different learning preferences within an integrated learning ecosystem.

Addresses: Material Diversity (21% gap)
03

Progressive Scaffolding

Implement scaffolded approaches to student autonomy that acknowledge post-pandemic variations in self-regulatory skills, beginning with structured guidance and gradually increasing student choice in learning pathways.

Addresses: Autonomy Concerns (58%)
04

Interdependent Components

Create seamless interconnections where online materials prepare conceptual foundations that in-class sessions build upon, while in-class experiences generate insights for subsequent online reflection and application.

Core BL Design Principle
05

Enhanced Collaborative Environments

Address the 22% wanting more collaboration by fostering robust communities of inquiry that support both cognitive and affective dimensions of engagement across online and in-person components.

Addresses: Collaboration Gap (22%)

Key Takeaway

Effective learning design is not about implementing a single model, but about creating flexible, responsive educational experiences that recognize and nurture the unique learning potential of each student. The validated inclusion-engagement interconnection demonstrates that addressing inclusion gaps through material diversification and collaborative opportunities could simultaneously enhance both cognitive and affective engagement dimensions.

Challenges with Self-Directed Learning

Students consistently reported difficulties with the self-directed nature of pre-class activities, particularly in statistics content where concepts build sequentially.

Key Findings:

  • Only 58% felt autonomous in independent learning
  • Sequential skill development in statistics creates unique challenges
  • Post-pandemic students exhibit greater variability in self-directed learning skills

Implications for BL Design:

BL redesign should incorporate progressive scaffolding as a core design principle, beginning with more structured guidance and gradually increasing student choice in learning pathways as comfort with statistical concepts develops.

Importance of Immediate Feedback

Focus group participants emphasized the need for timely guidance during pre-class activities, especially when encountering complex statistical concepts.

Key Findings:

  • Only 46% perceived adequate instructor-peer interaction opportunities
  • Students felt isolated during pre-class conceptual learning
  • Delayed feedback hindered understanding of foundational concepts

Implications for BL Design:

BL environments should integrate synchronous online discussion forums, virtual office hours, and peer collaboration tools as integral components rather than optional additions.

Learning Preference Diversity

Students expressed varied preferences for material presentation and learning pathways, reinforcing the finding that 21% found materials insufficiently diverse.

Key Findings:

  • Participants described different approaches based on prior mathematical background
  • 21% disagreed that materials were sufficiently diverse
  • Learning preferences varied significantly across student demographics

Implications for BL Design:

Effective BL design should develop multiple content formats—videos, interactive simulations, case studies, and written materials—that accommodate different learning preferences within an integrated learning ecosystem.
