Qatar Biobank Chronic Pain Study

This study applies Bayesian Network modeling to examine interdependencies among chronic pain locations and their relationships with age and gender. Using data from 2,400 adult participants, the model identifies direct and indirect associations among pain locations and demographic factors, quantified by odds ratios (ORs).

Key Clinical Insight

Combined hand and back pain elevates generalized pain probability to 37.9%, compared to just 6.6% with back pain alone (OR 7.68). This demonstrates how addressing "hub" pain sites early could prevent systemic pain propagation.

Clinical Implications & Strategies

Evidence-based intervention recommendations from network analysis

🎯 Hand Pain as Hub Node

Critical intervention target

  • OR 8.69 with hip pain - shared musculoskeletal pathways
  • OR 8.25 with foot pain - systemic sensitization
  • OR 7.68 with generalized pain - early intervention prevents spread

Recommendation: Prioritize early physiotherapy for hand pain

🧠 Facial Pain Dependencies

Neurological pathway indicator

  • OR 11.30 with headache - shared trigeminal pathways
  • OR 8.41 with hand pain - central sensitization
  • Prevalence: 0.7% → 7.6% with headache

Recommendation: Multimodal migraine management

Gender-Sensitive Strategies

Female Patients

  • 1.88x higher headache odds - hormonal evaluation
  • 2.54x higher hand pain odds - autoimmune markers
  • 70.1% of hand pain cases are female

Male Patients

  • 64.5% report no chronic pain - may underreport
  • Focus on localized musculoskeletal issues
  • Investigate occupational factors
