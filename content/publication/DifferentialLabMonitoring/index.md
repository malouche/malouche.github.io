abstract: >
  Laboratory monitoring in autoimmune disease is shaped as much by care
  pathways as by clinical need, and unequal monitoring intensity can bias any
  comparison of measured risk between conditions. Using matched case-control
  data from primary care in Qatar, this study quantifies differential
  laboratory monitoring across autoimmune conditions, characterises the
  patient and disease factors associated with monitoring intensity, and
  examines the extent to which apparent between-condition differences in
  laboratory abnormalities reflect surveillance rather than underlying
  pathology. The findings bear directly on how routinely collected primary-care
  laboratory data should be used in observational research.
