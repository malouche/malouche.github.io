### A bibliometric analysis of artificial intelligence and machine learning in preclinical mesenchymal stem cell therapy and manufacturing from 2009 to 2025

**Authors:** Aisha Al-Khinji, Dhafer Malouche

**Status:** Posted

**Abstract:** Mesenchymal stem/stromal cell therapies have approached the clinic for two decades, but potency and quality-control problems in cell manufacturing slow their translation, and artificial intelligence is increasingly proposed as a remedy. We mapped the intersection with a bibliometric analysis of 144 documents (95 original articles and 49 reviews, 2009 to 2025) retrieved from PubMed and enriched through OpenAlex and Crossref. Output grew at a compound annual rate of 35.1% and was overwhelmingly preclinical (47.9%); manufacturing and quality-control studies made up 13.2% of the corpus and clinical-stage work only 2.1%. The studies concentrated on cell characterization rather than release-grade potency testing. A reproducible corpus and analysis pipeline accompany the paper.

### Maternal age, not parity, and pregnancy loss: a Bayesian network analysis of the Qatar Biobank

**Authors:** Aisha Al-Khinji, Dhafer Malouche

**Status:** Posted

**Abstract:** Pregnancy loss reflects sociodemographic, reproductive and metabolic factors acting together, yet these are usually studied one at a time, particularly in Gulf populations. Using logistic regression and Bayesian network structure learning, we analyzed cross-sectional data on 864 ever-married women in the Qatar Biobank, of whom 304 (35.2%) reported at least one pregnancy loss. Maternal age was the dominant correlate: the odds of loss reached 4.44 (95% CI 2.06–9.57) at ages 61 and above relative to women aged 30 or younger, whereas parity lost its association after adjustment for age. In the network, age was the only variable sharing a direct edge with pregnancy loss (bootstrap support 89%). The design is cross-sectional, so all relationships are associational.

{{< icon name="file-pdf" pack="fas" >}} [Preprint on Research Square](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10463701/v1)

### Bayesian and frequentist meta-analysis of GCB-classification heterogeneity in TP53-reporting DLBCL cohorts at small k: a methodological exemplar

**Authors:** Aisha Al-Khinji, Hadeel Alashwal, Abdullatif Al-Hor, Hissa Al-Kuwari, Farwa Bismi, Nada Maki, Taghreed Abunada, Dhafer Malouche

**Status:** Published in *BMC Medical Research Methodology* (2026), under the title “Bayesian and frequentist meta-analysis of GCB-classification heterogeneity in TP53-reporting DLBCL cohorts at small k: an applied case study with reporting guidance”.

**Abstract:** Meta-analyses of biomarker prevalence in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma often rest on five or fewer cohorts, where heterogeneity statistics destabilize and priors begin to dominate posteriors. Using cohorts that report both TP53 status and cell-of-origin classification as a methodological exemplar (five cohorts, 738 patients), we targeted the probability of germinal-center B-cell-like (GCB) classification with a random-effects frequentist analysis and a hierarchical Bayesian analysis in Stan that share one estimand. Heterogeneity in the GCB proportion was meaningful but imprecisely estimated at k = 5, and the odds ratio for TP53 positivity versus GCB classification, reconstructible in three cohorts, was 0.82 (95% CI 0.53–1.26), consistent with no association. The analysis is exploratory, and future cohort studies should pre-specify joint TP53 and cell-of-origin reporting.

### A reproducible framework for creating validated health indicators from Middle Eastern biobank data

**Authors:** Aisha Al-Khinji, Dhafer Malouche

**Status:** Posted

**Abstract:** Standardized measurement of population health needs reproducible transformations from raw biobank data to internationally calibrated indicators, a step rarely taken for Middle Eastern cohorts. We built and validated such a framework on 2,400 Qatar Biobank participants, deriving 24 indicators in five domains from published clinical thresholds (EWGSOP2, WHO, ESC/ESH 2018, IDF, GLI-2012, GOLD and CANTAB). Obesity prevalence was 39.5% (95% CI 37.6–41.5) and central obesity 60.6% (58.6–62.5). Against measured blood pressure, self-reported hypertension showed 50.9% sensitivity and 88.9% specificity (κ = 0.28, PABAK = 0.72). The framework recovered the expected sex difference in grip strength (Cohen's d = 2.41) and supports cross-cohort comparison in Gulf-region settings.

### The Ethics and Governance of Agentic AI in Financial Markets: A Systematic Review and a Normative Framework for Responsible Deployment

**Authors:** Chaker Mzoughi, Dhafer Malouche

**Status:** Posted

**Abstract:** Agentic AI systems, capable of autonomous goal pursuit, adaptive learning and multi-agent coordination, are being deployed in financial markets faster than the frameworks meant to govern them. We report a systematic scoping review of 30 governance documents published between 2022 and 2025 across seven jurisdictions, coded against a 67-provision instrument and synthesized with a Bayesian Beta–Binomial model. Sixty-one percent of provisions are absent from the majority of the corpus, and transparency is the weakest dimension (estimated coverage 35%, 95% credible interval 30 to 40 percent). We propose a three-axis normative framework (autonomy level by market function by regulatory regime) and illustrate it with a case study of the Qatar Financial Centre.

### Dyslipidemia and Hypertension as Independent Risk Factors for Non-Alcoholic Steatohepatitis: A Matched Case-Control Study from Qatar

**Authors:** Aisha Al Khinji, Dhafer Malouche, Noof Al-Thani, Azza Mustafa, Jazeel Abdulmajeed, Mohamed Al-Kuwari

**Status:** Posted

**Abstract:** We estimated the independent associations of dyslipidemia and hypertension with non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) in a retrospective 1:1 age- and sex-matched case–control study of 894 cases and 894 controls drawn from Primary Health Care Corporation records in Qatar. Conditional logistic regression, which respects the matched design, gave adjusted odds ratios of 1.56 (95% CI 1.25–1.96) for dyslipidemia and 1.39 (95% CI 1.09–1.77) for hypertension. Both were independently associated with NASH, which supports targeted metabolic risk assessment in primary care.

### Probabilistic pathways from chronic pain to suicidal ideation in Qatar: a Bayesian-network study

**Authors:** Aisha Al Khinji, Dhafer Malouche

**Status:** Published in *Journal of Translational Medicine* 24, 899 (2026).

**Abstract:** Chronic pain frequently co-occurs with depressive and anxiety symptoms and doubles or triples the risk of suicidal ideation. Yet the joint pathways linking these conditions remain under-explored in Middle-Eastern settings. We analyzed questionnaire data from 2,363 Qatari adults enrolled in the Qatar Biobank using a data-driven Bayesian Network to map probabilistic interdependencies between chronic pain, mental-health symptoms and demographic factors, with a focus on sequences culminating in suicidal thoughts. The final BN identified self-regret and psychomotor change as the strongest direct predictors of suicidal thoughts, with an early-symptom cascade (sleep problems → anhedonia → depression) amplifying suicide risk. Fatigue linked chronic pain to self-regret, illustrating a physical–psychological bridge.

### Comparative Cardiovascular Risk Index Across Autoimmune Conditions in a Large Middle-East Health System

**Authors:** Aisha Al Khinji, Dhafer Malouche, Abdullatif Al-Hor, Hadeel Ashwal, et al.

**Status:** Posted

**Abstract:** Autoimmune diseases confer excess cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk, yet comparative profiles across phenotypes in Gulf health systems are unclear. Using routinely collected primary-care records, we analyzed 14,616 adults and derived a composite CVD risk index from a Framingham-based algorithm. Relative to the non-autoimmune reference, adjusted odds of high risk were higher in rheumatoid arthritis (OR 1.20, 95% CI 1.06–1.37), lower in Hashimoto's thyroiditis (0.49, 0.40–0.60), and not clearly different in SLE or multiple-autoimmune phenotypes. These findings support systematic CVD risk assessment and targeted management in autoimmune populations within Gulf health systems.

### Prediction markets doubt Le Pen's run-off more than her first round: polls and prices in the 2027 French presidential campaign

**Authors:** Dhafer Malouche

**Year:** 2026 — *SocArXiv*, doi:10.31235/osf.io/y9q5e

**Abstract:** Prediction markets and polls are usually compared as two forecasts of one event, but under a two-round rule they are not: a first-round poll measures intentions in one assumed line-up, a run-off poll one pairing, and a price on the presidency folds ballot access, the first round and the run-off into one number. Using a Bayesian state-space model of the polls and a model of Kalshi and Polymarket prices, we show that on 31 October 2026 the probability that Marine Le Pen reaches the run-off was 0.96 in the polls and 0.79 in the markets, and the probability that she wins it 0.89 against 0.42. The log ratio of the two splits exactly into ballot access, first round and run-off. The run-off carries 75 percent of the gap (90% interval 70 to 83), ballot access 17 and the first round 8: the markets doubt the run-off, not her first round. The analysis is exploratory; a registered evaluation follows the run-off of 2 May 2027.

### Beyond Psychological Needs: Integrating Self-Determination Theory and Communication Theory to Explain Feedback Effectiveness in Architectural Education

**Authors:** Daria Mizza, Tamer El Khouly, Dhafer Malouche, Oluwapelumi Dickson, Ilaria Compagnoni

**Year:** 2026 — *SSRN:6505578*

**Abstract:** Feedback practices in project-based courses may foster intrinsic motivation and informed academic choices, but intervention effects on the psychological needs for autonomy, competence and relatedness have been inconsistent. In a mixed-methods study of an undergraduate digital tools course in architecture across two semesters (N = 160), we examined how feedback supported these needs and whether support related to motivation and program declarations. Procedural autonomy was better supported than substantive autonomy, competence support fell from 91.5% positive for foundational skills to 30.5% for advanced ones, and relatedness was strong with instructors but weak among peers. Perceived need support strongly predicted intrinsic motivation (R² = 0.73), and focus groups identified four communication factors (message consistency, relational tone, interpersonal dynamics and feedback clarity) that explain why measured support diverged from lived experience.
